On 5th March, the governemnt's Online Safety Commissioner, Niamh Hodnett, announced the introduction of a new Digital Citizenship programme for 5th and 6th Class called [Switched On]('https://www.oidetechnologyineducation.ie/new-switched-on-programme-empowers-primary-pupils-to-become-safe-smart-and-responsible-digital-citizens/'). It aims to <i>"...empower primary pupils to become bafe, smart and responsible digital ditizens"</i>. This is a welcome addition to the primary school curriculum. In the previous article - [Social Media Bans for children - Not a Panacea]('/blog/bans') - I had highlighted the fact that Australia had introduced their ban on social media for under 16s before providing any details on the education the affected children would receive. I was critical of this approach, claiming it was putting the cart before the horse. It is good to see the Irish government setting out their education plans before any potential ban is brought in here.

In this article, I want to provide an independent, critical review of the Switched On programme. Before getting into the details, it is worth explaining what that means in plain terms. A critical review is not about being negative for the sake of it. It means looking carefully at something, asking what works, what doesn't, what might be missing, and what could be improved. The purpose is simple: make the final result better. So when I point out weaknesses, I am not attacking the people who created the programme, I am trying to help stress-test it. I am also trying to understand the gaps which can be addressed by the independent curriculum being developed here on Digital Knights.
