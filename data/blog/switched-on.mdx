On 5th March 2026, a new Digital Citizenship programme for 5th and 6th Class called [Switched On]('https://www.oidetechnologyineducation.ie/new-switched-on-programme-empowers-primary-pupils-to-become-safe-smart-and-responsible-digital-citizens/') was announced by the government's Online Safety Commissioner, Niamh Hodnett. It aims to <i>"...empower primary pupils to become safe, smart and responsible digital ditizens"</i>. Created by [Webwise](https://webwise.ie) and supported by Coimisiún na Meán, Switched On provides interactive educational content on a range of relevant topics. These include <i>"recognising misinformation and disinformation, staying secure online, managing screen time, and protecting personal information and privacy"</i>.

This is a welcome, and long overdue addition to the primary school curriculum. In my previous article - [Social Media Bans for children - Not a Panacea]('/blog/bans') - I highlighted the fact that Australia had introduced their ban on social media for under 16s before providing any details on the education the affected children would receive. I was critical of this approach, claiming it was putting the cart before the horse. It is good to see the Irish government setting out their education plans before any potential ban is brought in here.

After reviewing the content of the curriculum, I don't have full confidence that it adequately prepares children to become digital citizens. While some of the topics are very well covered, the content on 'staying secure' and 'protecting personal information and privacy' is relatively shallow. It provides very little effective information on these topics, and even contains information that could be misleading.

In this article, I provide an in depth review of the Switched On programme. The format of the review is as follows: An overview, followed by sections outlining The Good and the Bad. It is important to note that the purpose of the critical element of this review is not to dismiss the programme or those who create it, but to assess honestly where it succeeds, where it falls short, and what could be improved to better prepare children for life online.

#### No Practical Element

The greatest weakness of the course is the lack of any practical application of the lessons. For learning to be effective, putting theory into practice is vital. That is why it is a component in every respected educational framework. Among the common frameworks in use, I am personally fond of using David Merril's '5 Principles' (First Principle's of Instruction, 2002). Merril was a researcher who reviewed the content of a number of popular, existing frameworks to identify the commonalities. He distilled this down to 5 principles:

1. Task/Problem-centred
2. Activation (prior knowledge)
3. Demonstration (show, don't just tell)
4. Application (practice with feedback)
5. Integration (transfer to real world)

Let's use one of the topics covered in the Switched On curriculum and plan a lesson, then compare it to what is actually provided. For balance, I will also include an example where the Switched On curriculum does do a better job, again using Merril's 5 Principles to explain.

### The Ugly

Now for the ugly. You would be forgiven if you are wondering how this can get any uglier. The last section was . Well, it does. However, I feel I need to preface this section with a story that will hopefully illustrate why I have chosen to highlight the criticisms that follow below. Here goes...

Last year, my wife and I put in a bid on a house we both really liked. We viewed it three times, once accompanied by my father-in-law, a man who is more knowledegable in this area than either of us. All three of us were confident the current bid was a good bit below what the house was worth, and if we could get it at anywhere near that price, it would be a steal. After a few days, and with no further movement from other bidders, we were starting to get excited about this property becoming our forever home. However, the excitement was accompanied with a side of anxiety (or maybe 'bidder's regret') - why is no-one else bidding? Are there problems that we didn't notice? So, we decided to hire a builder to give it the once over and let us know if there were any issues.

The builder spent 30 minutes in the house, investigating every nook and cranny. He sat down with us afterwards and listed numerous issues that indicated the two extensions on the house were not constructed by professionals. These included the pitch of the conservatory roof being too shallow, incorrectly installed plumbing, and settlement of brickwork. He concluded that this was a DIY job by an amateur. Apparently something that is quite common for extensions completed in the 80s and 90s. If we bought the house, the extensions would all need to be completely rebuilt, and it would cost upwards of 300k.

We did not have 300k to spend on top of our bid, so we pulled out. But we learned a valuable lesson - get the experts in! That house was eventually bought by another party, who have since pulled down both extensions, rebuilt and put on a complete retrofit.

Ok, story over. How does this relate to 'Switched On'? Well, our builder was able to infer, from the quality of the construction work on the extensions, that the person/people who carried it out were not experts. There were numerous basic errors and oversights that skilled construction workers would not make. You would not want to live in a house constructed by the builder of these extensions- it would be costly and dangerous.
