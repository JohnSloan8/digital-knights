Hi, I'm John, and I want to change how we teach children in Ireland about tech and cybersecurity.

It's early 2026 and we are seeing social media bans for children being introduced across the world. Australia led the way in December, with a number of European countries, including France and Spain, quickly following suit. These actions have been taken in response to a growing awareness of the negative effects of social media on young children. Ireland looks set to join the list of governments putting stricter measures on use for under 16s into law.

What we are not currently seeing, amidst these announcemenets of bans, are details on the education the children affected are to receive prior to reaching this new age of digital consent. What are they to learn? At what age? For how long? Who will it be taught by?

The resources and curricula currently available here in Ireland for tech and digital safety fall significantly short of what I personally plan to teach my own children. Methods for minimising your digital footprint and controlling your own devices and data are among a range of topics which are key for privacy and security> These are not covered at all, or in sufficient detail, in the current materials.

To be fair to the creators of the existing content, it is difficult to design learning materials on technology for the general public. Most parents and teachers are not tech experts, and cannot be expected to learn the details of computing, networking, cryptography etc., in order to best prepare the next generation. There is also the general belief that technology is a complex and difficult topic that is beyond the learning capabilities of young kids.

I believe that children _can_ learn a huge amount about technology from a young age. I also believe that we do need people who understand the underlying technologies to be teaching children tech and online safety. If one wants to maintain privacy and security online, a certain minimum level of technical literacy is required. It is preferable for these skills to be learned before a child possesses their own internet enabled device and begins creating a permanent, identifiable record of all their online activities.

To this end, I am developing a curriculum with the aim of teaching my own children this topic in the future. I am interested in speaking to other parents and getting their input into the new learning materials. These will be made available here, on this site. I am also interested in teaching this content to children and parents.

If you are a parent and are interested in providing your input into the curriculum design, please fill out the [survey](/survey).

## About Me

My career has been spanned two fields - technology and education. Starting out as an ESL teacher in my early 20s travelling the world, I taught small groups of primary school children their ABCs. A return to academia - a Masters and PhD in Computer Science at UCD - followed. This led to a position as a Research Fellow with the [ABAIR](https://abair.ie) project, where I helped provide state-of-the-art Speech and Language Technologies for the Irish language to the general public. I specialised in infrastructure and cybersecurity, gaining ISC2's [CISSP](https://www.isc2.org/certifications/cissp) certification in the process. I also lectured for two years at TCD, with one spent as Assistant Professor of Speech and Language Technologies.
