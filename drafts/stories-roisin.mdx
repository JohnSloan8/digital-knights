_This article is part of a series of stories based on fictional characters and the effects of real technology._

It's lunch time at school, year 7. Roisín walks over to her friends who are laughing at what looks like a selfie Sarah has taken on her phone. It looks like a fairly standard photo, no funny expressions or joke captions. Sarah clocks Roisín's not in on the joke so she thrusts the phone closer,

"see?", she questions.

Roisín noticed the red marks outlining different areas of Sarah's face and a list at the bottom titled, "recommendations":

"rhinoplasty - high"

"chin augmentation - medium"

"A nose job!?"

"It's only a bit of fun. Here look, I uploaded all of our photos. Here's yours."

And there was her photo, cropped from a group shot they had all taken a few days before. Only this time the red lines were in different places, and the recommendations read

"lip filler - high"

"cheek implants - low"

Roisín shook her head, laughed and pouted her lips like a fish,

"I'll just go round like this from now on then I suppose."

With that, the bell rang and it was on to Irish class.

---

That night, after brushing her teeth before bed, Roisín looked straight-faced at her reflection in the mirror. Were her lips a bit on the thin side? She'd never noticed anything amiss previously. On the app on Sarah's phone there was a little down arrow next to each of the recommendations - more information on what the problem was with her lips, perhaps?

Roisín took her phone to bed, downloaded the app and uploaded another photograph, this one taken at a different angle with different lighting. But there it was again,

"recommendations"

"lip filler - high"

She tapped the little arrow and up popped a paragraph of text:

"Based on your photo analysis, enhancing lip volume with dermal fillers could help balance your facial features and add definition to your smile. Fuller lips often contribute to a more youthful and refreshed appearance by providing subtle contour and hydration. This minimally invasive procedure can be customized to achieve a natural look that complements your unique facial structure."

"balance my facial features...a more youthful look" she muttered while tapping the Google Chrome icon ready to search:

"What does lip filler look like?"

Up pops a row of before and after images of mostly young women who seem to go from being plain and tired looking to becoming quite beautiful and vibrant. Roisín scrolls down to find snippets of information she seeks.

"low risk of complications or side effects" (https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/22133-lip-fillers)

"enhance the beauty of your lips, restoring a more youthful appearance." (https://www.cosmeticskinclinic.com/blog/achieving-natural-looking-lip-augmentation-results-using-dermal-fillers/)

Surgery is something Roisín was not seriously considering. She was well aware that it was not available to under 18s. Anyhow, the app was meant for 18+ and it was telling her, a 13 year old, that she needed surgery to look younger. It was madness.

But when Roisín went to the bathroom the next morning to brush her teeth, her eyes were immediately drawn to her lips. They definitely were a bit on the thin side, not as full as Eloise or Fia's. But she had been taught that her physical appearance was not the most important thing, and she was old enough and wise enough to know this was correct.

Weeks passed, months went by and the gang had all but forgotten about the plastic surgery recommender app. Roisín would now always slightly purse her lips in photographs, and she hated cathching herself unawares in a mirror - that was when her defect would really stand out.

It wasn't until a couple of years later that Roisín's parents noticed anything different about her behaviour. She was at the stage where the realisation occurs that adulthood is only just around the corner and with it all the freedoms she could wish for. The talk was all about the summer after leaving cert and before College. Sarah's older sister and friends had just come back from XXXX and could stop raving about it. The girls were dead set on doing the same! Only small problem was the cost - the bank of mum and dad were definitely not giving loans for a wekk of partying abroad.

It was decided that they would all get jobs for June and July, then chill out and holiday in August. Roisín had another plan. She hadn't told her parents yet, but when she turned 18 and saved up some money from her job, she was going to get lip filler.

Reality

Searching for information on a search engine on any cosmetic preocedure brings up almost exclusively businesses selling the procedures (see your own search: https://www.google.com/search?q=what+does+lip+filler+look+like)
