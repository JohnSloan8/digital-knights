Education is the most powerful tool we have to tackle the problem we currently face regarding children accessing the internet.
What shape this education should take is still an open question.
We are still in a period where the problem, or set of problems, are only now being clearly defined.
Widespread use of smartphones and social media are only in their second decade.
Use of AI modelling is still in its first.
This means that it is only recently that problems associated with smartphones and social media have become apparent.
It may be many years before as yet unknown issues with AI modelling becomes clear

So, what are the problems we face now that can be tackled by education, and how can we begin thinking about
Most prominent of these are activity tacking, profiling, targetted content and advertising,  
There are three important skills which all children should acquire before venturing online.
These skills can be broadly categorised into the following groups:

- technical
- behavioural
- emotional

Before diving into specific details on the three skills, it would be useful to first understand the situation we currently face.

1. A new technology emerges which changes how people interact.
2. This change is widely perceived as positive and beneficial.
3. Some time later, significant problems become apparent.
4. Removing the technology to solve the problems is not faesible.
5. Education becomes an important method for mitigating the problem.

A useful analogy for this comes from the public health domain - accessibility to the oral contraceptive pill in the 1960s.
This analogy is useful for three main reasons: it closely mirrors the current situation; it has been going for much longer so we can see what the outcomes are; and, most importantly, these outcomes have mostly been very successful.

So, what are the similarities between accessibility to the internet in the 1990s and the oral contraceptive pill in the 1960s?

In the 1960s, The oral contraceptive pill first became available in the USA.
It gave people more control over reproductive health and the ability to plan their own lives accordingly.
THis led to an increase in the number of women entering third level education in the 1970s, and delaying marriage to invest in their careers [1].

The problem that arose with use of the contraceptive pill relates to the prevalence of Sexually Transmitted Diseases (STDs).
STDs steadily increased across the USA in the 1960s [2].

1. Goldin, C., & Katz, L. F. (2002). The power of the pill: Oral contraceptives and women’s career and marriage decisions. Journal of political Economy, 110(4), 730-770.
